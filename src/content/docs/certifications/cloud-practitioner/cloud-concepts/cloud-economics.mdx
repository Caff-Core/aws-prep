## The idea in plain language

Buying a car means a large payment up front, then insurance and servicing whether you drive or not.
Using e-hailing means paying for each trip. If you drive every day, one may suit you. If you travel
occasionally, the other may. The total depends on how you use it.

Cloud economics works the same way. It changes **when** you pay, **what** you pay for, and **who**
carries the work of running the equipment.

## Fixed costs and variable costs

A **fixed cost** stays the same whether you use something or not. A **variable cost** changes with how
much you use.

Buying servers is a fixed cost. You pay for them even when they sit idle. The AWS Cloud lets you trade
that for variable costs, so you pay for the IT you actually consume.

<ImportantConcept>
  Moving to variable costs lowers the risk of guessing wrong. If a project fails, you stop using the
  resources instead of being left with equipment you bought.
</ImportantConcept>

## Costs of running on-premises

The price of a server is only part of the cost of running your own environment. You also carry:

- Data center space, power, and cooling.
- Networking equipment.
- Buying, installing, and replacing hardware.
- Software installation and patching.
- Staff time to do all of the above.

When you use AWS, it takes on the physical side of this work. How much else it takes on depends on the
service. For example, with Amazon Relational Database Service (Amazon RDS), AWS also handles database
software patching and backups.

## Economies of scale

**Economies of scale** means that cost per unit falls as size grows. AWS combines usage from a very
large number of customers, which lets it offer lower pay-as-you-go prices than most organisations could
reach by themselves.

Eligibility depends on the terms of your license. For example, Amazon EC2 **Dedicated Hosts** give you
a physical server for your own use, which supports licenses tied to sockets or cores. Check your
license terms before you plan to bring it.

## Rightsizing

**Rightsizing** means matching resources to what a workload actually needs. A server that rarely uses
its capacity is costing more than necessary. Moving it to a smaller size reduces the bill without
hurting the workload.

**AWS Compute Optimizer** analyses configuration and usage data, then recommends rightsizing changes
and identifies idle resources.

<CostWarning title="Cost warning: savings are not automatic">
  Moving to the cloud does not reduce costs by itself. Oversized or idle resources keep costing
  money until someone changes them.
</CostWarning>

## The benefits of automation

Automation means letting software do repeatable work. In cloud economics, its value comes from less
manual effort and fewer mistakes. Examples from earlier lessons:

- Amazon EC2 Auto Scaling adjusts capacity without anyone watching a dashboard.
- AWS DMS manages the hardware and software that a migration needs.

Staff time saved can go to work that helps the business. AWS lists staff productivity as part of the
value of the cloud, alongside cost savings, operational resilience, business agility, and
sustainability.

<RealWorldExample title="Real-world example: a retailer's first year">
  A Johannesburg retailer moves its online store to AWS. It no longer plans a hardware refresh,
  which removes a large fixed cost. After a month, Compute Optimizer shows that several servers are
  oversized, so the team rightsizes them. Staff who used to replace disks now work on the store
  itself.
</RealWorldExample>

## What the exam expects

<ExamTip>
  **Paying for idle equipment** points to fixed costs. **Already owns licenses** points to BYOL.
  **Oversized resources** points to rightsizing. **Lower prices because of many customers** points
  to economies of scale.
</ExamTip>

## Common misconceptions

- **"Moving to AWS removes all IT costs."** You still pay for what you use, and you still manage your
  applications and data.
- **"Cloud cost only means the monthly bill."** A fair comparison includes on-premises costs such as
  power, cooling, hardware replacement, and staff time.
- **"Any license can move to AWS."** Eligibility depends on the license terms.

## Try it yourself (optional)

You need no AWS account for this, and it costs nothing. List everything needed to run one server in an
office for three years, including power, cooling, space, and staff time. Mark each item as a fixed or
variable cost. Most people find more items than they expect.

## Summary

<LessonSummary>

- The cloud trades fixed costs for variable costs.
- On-premises costs include space, power, cooling, hardware, and staff time.
- Economies of scale let AWS offer lower pay-as-you-go prices.
- BYOL uses eligible licenses you own. With license included, the price covers the license.
- Rightsizing matches resources to real needs, and automation reduces manual effort.

</LessonSummary>
