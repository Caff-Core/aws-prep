## The idea in plain language

Managing household money takes three habits. You estimate costs before a big purchase. You set a limit
and get warned when you near it. You look back at your statements to see where the money went.

AWS has a tool for each habit.

### AWS Pricing Calculator

**AWS Pricing Calculator** is a free web tool for estimating the cost of services and architectures before
you build them. You can share estimates or export them.

### AWS Budgets

**AWS Budgets** tracks cost and usage against targets you set. Alerts can fire on actual or forecasted
spend, and they arrive by email or through Amazon SNS. Budget actions can respond automatically, such as
applying an IAM policy when a threshold is reached.

### AWS Cost Explorer

**AWS Cost Explorer** lets you view and analyse your costs. You can look back up to 13 months, forecast
future spend, and receive Reserved Instance recommendations.

<ImportantConcept>
  Budgets **warns** you. Cost Explorer **explains** what happened. Pricing Calculator **estimates**
  before you start.
</ImportantConcept>

## AWS Organizations and consolidated billing

**AWS Organizations** centrally manages many AWS accounts, grouped into organizational units. Service
control policies provide guardrails across accounts.

**Consolidated billing** means the management account pays the charges of all member accounts. Its
benefits are:

- **One bill** for all accounts.
- **Combined usage**, which shares volume pricing discounts, Reserved Instance discounts, and Savings
  Plans benefits.
- **No extra fee** for the feature.

## Cost allocation tags

A **tag** is a key and value pair attached to a resource, such as `Project=Website`. **Cost allocation
tags** let you track costs by project, team, or anything else that matters to you.

Both types must be activated before they appear in cost tools. Do not put sensitive information in tags.

## Billing reports

**AWS Cost and Usage Reports** are the most comprehensive cost and usage data, delivered to an Amazon S3
bucket and able to include your tags. AWS Data Exports now offers a newer version, CUR 2.0, which AWS
recommends. Reports can be queried with Amazon Athena.

<RealWorldExample title="Real-world example: a marketing agency">
  A Pretoria agency runs each client's website in a separate account under AWS Organizations, so it
  receives one bill. It tags resources by client and reviews costs in Cost Explorer. A budget alert
  warns the team when any client approaches its monthly limit.
</RealWorldExample>

<CostWarning title="Cost warning: set a budget before you experiment">
  Before trying AWS, create a budget alert. New accounts choose a free plan with credits or a paid
  plan, and the terms change, so read the current AWS Free Tier page. Never assume a service is
  free.
</CostWarning>

## What the exam expects

<ExamTip>
  **Estimate before building** means Pricing Calculator. **Alert when spending passes a threshold**
  means AWS Budgets. **Analyse past spending** means Cost Explorer. **One bill for many accounts**
  means consolidated billing. **Track cost by project** means cost allocation tags.
</ExamTip>

## Common misconceptions

- **"Budgets stops spending automatically."** By default, it alerts you. Budget actions must be set up.
- **"Tags work in reports immediately."** They must be activated first.
- **"Consolidated billing costs extra."** It has no additional fee.

## Try it yourself (optional)

You can do this without an AWS account, and it costs nothing. Open
[AWS Pricing Calculator](https://calculator.aws/) and estimate a small website with one server. Change
the Region and see how the estimate moves. Estimates exclude taxes.

## Summary

<LessonSummary>

- AWS Pricing Calculator estimates, AWS Budgets alerts, and AWS Cost Explorer analyses.
- AWS Organizations manages many accounts, and consolidated billing gives one bill.
- Combined usage shares discounts across accounts.
- Cost allocation tags track costs and must be activated.
- Cost and Usage Reports give the most detailed data.

</LessonSummary>
