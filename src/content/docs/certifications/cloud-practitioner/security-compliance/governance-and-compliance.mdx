## The idea in plain language

A restaurant has to show a health inspector that it follows the rules. It keeps certificates on the
wall, records fridge temperatures, and logs who received each delivery.

Organisations in the cloud have similar duties. **Compliance** means meeting the laws, regulations, and
standards that apply to you. **Governance** means the rules and oversight an organisation uses to run
its systems responsibly. AWS provides documents and services that support both.

## Finding compliance information

**AWS Artifact** gives you on-demand access to AWS security and compliance documents, such as ISO
certifications, Payment Card Industry (PCI) reports, and System and Organization Controls (SOC) reports.
You can also review and track agreements with AWS. The documents are free to download.

If an auditor asks for proof of how AWS controls its side of the shared responsibility model, AWS
Artifact is where to go.

## Why compliance needs vary

Requirements depend on where you operate, your industry, and the services you use. AWS groups its
compliance programs into three areas.

A health company in the United States considers the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act
(HIPAA). A company handling data about people in the European Union considers the General Data
Protection Regulation (GDPR). Compliance requirements also vary among AWS services, so check that the
services you plan to use are covered by the programs you need.

<ImportantConcept>
  AWS certifications cover the AWS side. Customers remain responsible for their own compliance with
  the laws that apply to them. AWS Artifact does not produce your company's compliance documents.
</ImportantConcept>

## Monitoring, auditing, and reporting

The exam guide names these services by role. Learn each by the question it answers.

### Amazon CloudWatch: monitoring

**Amazon CloudWatch** monitors AWS resources and applications through metrics, alarms, dashboards, and
logs. It answers the question "How is my system performing right now?"

### AWS CloudTrail: auditing actions

**AWS CloudTrail** records actions taken by users, roles, and services as events. It answers the
question "Who did what, and when?" You can view the last 90 days of management events in event history,
and you can create a trail to deliver logs to Amazon S3 for longer storage.

### AWS Config: auditing configuration

**AWS Config** records how your resources are configured over time, and how they relate to each other.
Its rules flag resources that do not comply with the settings you want. It answers the question "What
did this resource look like, and does it follow our rules?"

### Access reports

Two reports support least-privilege reviews:

- An **IAM credential report** lists users and the status of their passwords, access keys, and
  multi-factor authentication devices.
- **IAM last accessed information** helps you find permissions that are not being used, so that you can
  remove them.

<RealWorldExample title="Real-world example: an insurer's audit">
  An insurer in Sandton prepares for an audit. Staff download AWS reports from AWS Artifact to show
  the controls on the AWS side. They use AWS CloudTrail to show who changed a sensitive setting, and
  AWS Config to show that storage settings stayed compliant over the year.
</RealWorldExample>

## What the exam expects

<ExamTip>
  **AWS compliance reports** point to AWS Artifact. **Who made this API call** points to AWS
  CloudTrail. **Resource configuration history** points to AWS Config. **Metrics and alarms** point
  to Amazon CloudWatch.
</ExamTip>

## Common misconceptions

- **"CloudTrail and CloudWatch do the same job."** CloudTrail records actions. CloudWatch monitors
  performance and resources.
- **"Using a certified provider makes me compliant."** Customers still have their own duties.
- **"Every AWS service meets every standard."** Requirements vary among services.

## Try it yourself (optional)

You need no AWS account for this, and it costs nothing. Read the
[AWS Compliance Programs](https://aws.amazon.com/compliance/programs/) page and find one program that
applies to your country or industry. Note whether it is a certification, a law, or a framework.

- IAM credential reports and last accessed information support access reviews.
