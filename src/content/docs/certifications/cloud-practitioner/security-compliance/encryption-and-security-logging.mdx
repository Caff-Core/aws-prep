## The idea in plain language

Think about valuables and money. In a safe at home, they are protected while they sit still. In an
armoured van, they are protected while they move. You need both, because a thief can strike at either
point.

Data is the same. **Encryption** scrambles data so that it can be read only with a secret key. Even if
someone gets hold of encrypted data, they cannot understand it without the key. That is the benefit of
encryption: it protects data even when another safeguard fails.

## Encryption at rest and in transit

**Encryption at rest** protects stored data, such as files on a disk or objects in a storage service.

**Encryption in transit** protects data while it moves between systems. On the web, this usually means
Transport Layer Security (TLS), the technology behind the padlock in your browser. It is often still
called SSL/TLS.

<ImportantConcept>
  Under the shared responsibility model, AWS provides encryption features. For many services, the
  customer chooses the encryption options and controls who can use the keys.
</ImportantConcept>

## Services that manage keys and certificates

### AWS Key Management Service

**AWS Key Management Service (AWS KMS)** lets you create and control the keys that encrypt your data.
Key policies decide who can use each key. Services that use KMS keys record each use in AWS CloudTrail,
so that you can audit it.

### AWS Certificate Manager

**AWS Certificate Manager (ACM)** creates, stores, and renews the public and private SSL/TLS
certificates that secure connections. A website uses a certificate to prove its identity and to encrypt
traffic. ACM certificates carry no extra charge.

## What the exam expects

<ExamTip>
  **Stored data** means at rest. **Data moving across a network** means in transit. **Encryption
  keys** point to AWS KMS. **SSL/TLS certificates** point to AWS Certificate Manager.
</ExamTip>

## Common misconceptions

- **"Encryption at rest also protects data on the network."** It protects stored data. Data moving
  across a network needs encryption in transit.
- **"Encryption replaces access control."** It adds protection. You still need to control who can reach
  your data and keys.
- **"AWS KMS stores my files."** It manages keys. Your data lives in storage services.

## Try it yourself (optional)

You need no AWS account for this, and it costs nothing. Visit a website over HTTPS and open the padlock
or site information in your browser. Find who issued its certificate and when it expires. You are looking
at encryption in transit at work.

## Summary

<LessonSummary>

- Encryption protects data even if someone gets hold of it.
- Encryption at rest protects stored data. Encryption in transit protects moving data.
- AWS KMS manages encryption keys. AWS Certificate Manager manages SSL/TLS certificates.
- AWS CloudTrail, CloudWatch Logs, AWS Config, and VPC Flow Logs capture security-related records.

</LessonSummary>
